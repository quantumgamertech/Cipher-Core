# Customer Journey

## 1. Introduction

Cipher opens with calm presence and a plain-language prototype label. It explains that configuration is local and nothing is connected.

## 2. Identity

The customer chooses a public identity, then names the teammate. The chosen name is theirs; Cipher does not force a fixed face letter or persona name.

## 3. Purpose

The customer selects a focused role. Cipher explains what that role could eventually prepare or request, without implying current capability.

## 4. Business context

The customer previews business name, industry, hours, services, policies, FAQs, lead preferences, and tone. Real products will require data-isolation and retention controls before saving.

## 5. Review

Cipher summarizes identity, name, purpose, and context. Missing information is visible. Permissions remain off.

## 6. Readiness

“I'm ready” completes the prototype and returns a profile preview. It does not connect accounts, save customer data, or activate actions.

## 7. Ongoing trust

Future customers must always see connection health, active permissions, pending approvals, recent actions, and ways to pause or revoke access.
